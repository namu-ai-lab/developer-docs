## Tell people it is AI

When your app speaks with a Namu voice, let listeners know the voice is generated, the first time they hear it and wherever it matters: health, money, official or legal information. A short line is enough: *"This message is read by a computer voice."*

## Ask before you listen

Recordings sent to `/v1/transcribe`, `/v1/translate` and `/v1/converse` are people's voices and words. Translation and the voice agent pass the text they work on to Google Gemini through OpenRouter. Tell people their voice is sent to a service to be understood, get their agreement, and do not send recordings of people who did not agree, such as voice notes forwarded from others.

## Do not use it to deceive or harm

Do not use the API to:

- **impersonate** a real person, an official or an organisation, or make people believe they hear someone they do not;
- **defraud**, for example fake calls or messages about mobile money, prizes or debts;
- **mislead people about elections**, public health or emergencies;
- **harass, threaten or shame** anyone;
- **make decisions about people** (health, credit, jobs, justice) from a transcript or translation without a person checking it.

## Keep a person where mistakes hurt

Speech recognition and translation make mistakes, more often with noise, names and numbers. Where a mistake could hurt someone, show the text back, ask for confirmation, or have a person check.

## Respect the languages

Have native speakers listen to the audio and read the transcripts your app produces before launch. Hausa, Zarma and French speakers will notice what a test script cannot, and they will tell you, and us, how to do better.

## Report misuse

If you see the API used to deceive or harm, write to [contact@namuai.org](mailto:contact@namuai.org).
