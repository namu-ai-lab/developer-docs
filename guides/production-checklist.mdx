## Keys and security

- [ ] The key lives on your server, read from an environment variable or secret store. It is not in the app, the web page or the repository.
- [ ] Each app and environment has its own key, with only the scopes it uses.
- [ ] You know how to [revoke a key](/guides/api-keys#revoke-a-key) and who on your team can do it.
- [ ] Your server checks your own users before calling the API on their behalf, so a stranger cannot use your server to spend your allowance.

## Reliability

- [ ] Timeouts are generous (minutes), because the first request after a quiet spell is slower.
- [ ] Network errors, `429 rate_limited` and `5xx` are retried with a growing pause; `4xx` errors are not.
- [ ] Recordings are kept until their answer arrives, so a failed upload never asks someone to speak again.
- [ ] Your logs keep the `request_id` of failed requests (and not the audio or the text).

## Cost and limits

- [ ] Audio played to many people is generated once and stored.
- [ ] You check [`/v1/usage`](/guides/usage-and-rate-limits#check-your-usage) and know what happens in your app when `quota_exceeded` arrives.
- [ ] If you expect more than the beta allowance, you have [written to us](/resources/support) before launch.

## People

- [ ] Listeners know the voice is AI-generated.
- [ ] People know their voice is sent to a service to be transcribed or translated, and agreed to it.
- [ ] Where a mistake could hurt (health, money, legal), a person checks what the model produced.
- [ ] Native speakers of your users' language listened to your app's audio and read its transcripts before launch.

See [responsible use](/resources/responsible-use) for what the API may not be used for.
